# Contradictory In-Institution Clinical Artifacts: Tapering Chart vs Dispensing Label

## Problem Pattern
When a patient receives conflicting timing or dosing instructions from the **same medical institution** (e.g. hospital/specialist institute):
1. **Titration / Tapering Schedule (Chart):** Specially crafted by the clinician/medical team with specific spacing (e.g., Dexa BD at 8:00 AM & 2:00 PM — 6-hour spacing).
2. **Dispensing Packet Label (Pharmacy Envelope):** Automatically generated by the hospital pharmacy dispensing software (e.g., PhIS / SPUB) when a generic frequency code (e.g., "BD") is entered, defaulting to standard preset timings (e.g., 8:00 AM & 12:00 PM).

## User Concern & Epistemic Trap
- **Recency bias:** User may reasonably argue that the physical packet in hand is the "most recent" physical artifact and should override older printed charts.
- **Institutional contradiction:** Both artifacts come from the same hospital/clinic, causing legitimate confusion.
- **Agent failure mode:** Dismissing the packet label casually, or guessing/assuming one is strictly a mistake without verifying the pharmacological safety boundary.

## Grounded Protocol

### 1. Root-Cause Distinction (Mechanics vs Dispensing Defaults)
- Explain the distinct origins:
  - **Clinical Titration Chart:** Customized for pharmacokinetics (e.g., daytime steroid coverage, preventing insomnia by avoiding late evening doses, maintaining consistent 6h therapeutic intervals).
  - **Pharmacy Dispensing Label:** Automated system output mapping `BD` -> `Pagi & Tengah Hari (8am & 12pm)` unless custom free-text instructions are manually keyed in.

### 2. Pharmacological & Safety Assessment
- Evaluate interval spacing:
  - 8am & 12pm = 4h interval with a 20h overnight gap.
  - 8am & 2pm = 6h interval with an 18h overnight gap (more balanced daytime coverage).
- Evaluate acute risk: Both timings keep the steroid intake within daytime hours (before 6pm) to mitigate sleep disruption/insomnia. Neither timing creates acute toxicity.

### 3. Non-Assumption Policy & Operational Stance
- **Never claim certainty without clinical confirmation:** Do not declare the pharmacy label "wrong" or the chart "outdated" as a settled absolute.
- **Operational Baseline:** Maintain the tailored titration schedule (8am & 2pm) in tracking/reminders as the primary protocol design.
- **Explicit Escalation:** Instruct the user to bring both physical artifacts (envelope and chart) to the next pharmacy/doctor review to obtain a definitive reconciliation and request re-labeled envelopes if necessary.
