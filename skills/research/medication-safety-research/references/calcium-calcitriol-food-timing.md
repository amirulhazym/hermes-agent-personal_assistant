# Calcium Carbonate & Calcitriol (CC) Food and Timing Protocol

## 1. Drug Characteristics & Food Requirements

### Calcium Carbonate (e.g. 500mg)
- **Mechanism:** Requires stomach acid (gastric HCl) for dissolution into soluble, absorbable ionic calcium salts ($Ca^{2+}$).
- **Food Requirement:** Does **NOT** require a heavy, oily, or full meal. A light snack (bread, biscuit, banana, or a small glass of milk) is sufficient to trigger cephalic and gastric phase acid secretion.
- **Empty Stomach:** Taking on a completely empty stomach reduces fractional absorption and may cause localized GI irritation, constipation, or gas.

### Calcitriol (Active 1,25-(OH)2D3, 0.25mcg)
- **Mechanism:** Biologically active form of Vitamin D; does not require hepatic 25-hydroxylation or renal 1-alpha-hydroxylation.
- **Food Requirement:** Official FDA label / prescribing info states it may be taken **with or without food**. While fat-soluble, it is formulated in solubilized vehicle (e.g., medium-chain triglycerides) and does not strictly require high-fat meals for clinical bioavailability.

## 2. Nighttime / Bedtime Administration
- **Safety:** Safe to take at night or before bed.
- **Precaution:** Avoid taking directly on a completely empty stomach right before lying flat to prevent gastric reflux or indigestion. A light snack is recommended.
- **Bone Resorption:** Nocturnal calcium administration can help suppress nighttime parathyroid hormone (PTH) surges and bone resorption.

## 3. Missed / Delayed Dose Decision Framework
- **Supportive vs Critical:** In steroid-induced osteoporosis regimens, CC is supportive. Missing or delaying a single dose by several hours has a **low acute risk** (no withdrawal, no seizure risk, no antimicrobial resistance risk).
- **Default Recommendation:** If taking late at night, take with a light snack rather than skipping completely, unless the user prefers to skip.
