# Food-effect PK fact-check reference

Use this reference when a medication-timing answer is challenged with a mechanistic explanation about fat, gastric emptying, or “bioavailability.” It is a worked evidence pattern, not a substitute for checking the current product label.

## Worked rifampicin/isoniazid example

### Official administration anchor

WHO WHOPAR Part 4 for a rifampicin/isoniazid-containing FDC states: “given as a single daily dose on an empty stomach (at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal). Absorption may be reduced if taken with food.” This supports a 2-hour-after-meal operational rule; it does not establish a universal 3-hour delay for every fatty intake.

DailyMed rifampin labeling states that absorption is reduced by about 30% when rifampin is ingested with food. DailyMed isoniazid labeling says isoniazid should not be administered with food and that bioavailability is reduced significantly.

### Controlled PK studies — preserve endpoint names

- Peloquin et al., rifampin, PMID 9925057 / DOI 10.1378/chest.115.1.12: in 14 volunteers, an FDA high-fat meal reduced rifampin `Cmax` by 36%, nearly doubled `Tmax` (about 2.42 to 4.43 hours), but reduced `AUC0-inf` by only 6%.
- Peloquin et al., isoniazid, PMID 10460103: in 14 volunteers, the high-fat meal reduced isoniazid `Cmax` by 51%, nearly doubled `Tmax`, and reduced `AUC0-inf` by 12%.

Interpretation: “Cmax down 51%” is not the same claim as “bioavailability down 51%.” State both endpoints and do not silently substitute one for the other.

### Gastric-emptying context

A human study used 19 g olive oil with 200 g carrot in 13 overweight patients with type 2 diabetes and found a lower acetaminophen-based gastric-emptying rate than the carrot control. The paper also reports that a previous study using the same approximately 20 mL olive-oil dose in healthy subjects found no clear gastric-emptying effect. This supports “possible context-dependent delay,” not a universal 2–5 g CCK threshold or proof that a user's stomach remains non-empty after exactly 3 hours.

### Pantoprazole claim boundary

The Pfizer pantoprazole label reports mean acid inhibition of 51% by 2.5 hours after an initial 40 mg dose and 85% after 7 days of once-daily dosing. This is not evidence for “acid stabilises after 3 hours.” Check for a direct PPI–TB interaction separately; absence from a label is not proof of zero interaction, and a secondary interaction checker may report rifampin reducing pantoprazole exposure rather than pantoprazole reducing rifampin exposure.

### Citation-integrity pitfall

The rifampin PK paper is PMID 9925057. PMID 9925056 is a different Chest paper on ventilator-associated pneumonia. Always verify the identifier/title pair before repeating a pasted citation.

### User-facing decision pattern

For a one-off intake at an approximate time:

1. Treat the latest calorie-containing intake as the timing anchor.
2. Apply the official food interval first.
3. If the time is approximate, use a modest buffer that remains inside the live regimen window and preserves the next dose gap.
4. Do not claim identical personal serum exposure; say the timing follows the labelled rule and is expected to be better than taking the TB dose with food.
5. If red-flag symptoms exist, escalate clinically rather than solving the problem with increasingly long timing delays.
