# Supply Gap: Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6) for INH Prophylaxis

## Scenario
- Patient on TB treatment (Akurit-4: INH+RIF+PZA+EMB)
- Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6, 3 tablets daily) ran out
- Next IPR appointment is tomorrow (1 day gap)
- Sunday — pharmacies open but specialist clinic closed

## Risk Assessment: Low

**Evidence:** MSD Manual Professional Edition (TB Treatment, Feb/May 2026 review):
> "Peripheral neuropathy can result from INH-induced pyridoxine (vitamin B6) deficiency... Pyridoxine 25 to 50 mg orally once/day can prevent this complication, but pyridoxine is usually not needed in children and healthy young adults."

**Why low risk:** INH-induced peripheral neuropathy is a **cumulative effect** — it develops over weeks to months of pyridoxine deficiency, not from 1-2 missed doses. The neuropathy onset is gradual and dose-dependent.

**Contrast:** This is NOT like missing:
- Dexamethasone (risk of adrenal insufficiency with abrupt cessation)
- Levetiracetam (risk of breakthrough seizures)
- Core TB drugs INH/RIF/PZA/EMB (risk of resistance/treatment failure)

## Bridging Options

| Option | Availability | Cost | Recommended? |
|--------|-------------|------|-------------|
| Vitamin B6 OTC (25mg or 50mg) | Guardian, Watsons, Caring | RM10-20 | ✅ Best for 1-2 day gap |
| Skip until IPR appointment | — | RM0 | ✅ Also safe (low risk) |
| Call IPR for emergency supply | Unlikely on Sunday | — | ❌ Not practical |

**Dosage alignment:** Standard INH prophylaxis dose is 25-50mg daily. If OTC B6 is 25mg, 1 tablet daily is sufficient. If 50mg, still within safe range (B6 is water-soluble, excess excreted).
