# TB Medication Timing — Evidence Summary

## Drugs Covered
- **Akurit-2**: Fixed-dose combination of Rifampin, Isoniazid, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol
- **Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6)**: Taken alongside Akurit to prevent isoniazid-induced neuropathy

## Pharmacokinetics (Source: UCSF Curry TB Center medication fact sheets)

| Drug | Half-life | Peak concentration | Oral absorption |
|------|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Rifampin | 7-8 hours | 1-4 hours post-dose | Good (90% bioavailable) |
| Isoniazid | 6-10 hours | 1-4 hours post-dose | Well absorbed |
| Pyrazinamide | ~10 hours | 1-4 hours post-dose | Well absorbed |

With half-lives of 6-10 hours, a 50-minute delay in dosing does not materially change steady-state drug concentrations.

## Official Missed-Dose Guidance

### Mayo Clinic (Rifampin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide combination)
> "If you miss a dose of this medicine, take it as soon as possible. However, if it is almost time for your next dose, skip the missed dose and go back to your regular dosing schedule. Do not double doses."
Source: https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements/rifampin-isoniazid-and-pyrazinamide-oral-route/description/drg-20062768

### MedlinePlus (Rifampin)
> "If you do miss a dose, take the missed dose as soon as you remember it. However, if it is almost time for the next dose, skip the missed dose and call your doctor. Do not take a double dose to make up for a missed one."
Source: https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/meds/a682403.html

### CDC (Inactive TB treatment)
> "If you forget to take your pills one day, skip that dose and take the next scheduled dose. Tell your health care provider that you missed a dose."
Source: https://www.cdc.gov/tb/treatment/inactive-tuberculosis.html

### MSF Medical Guidelines on Treatment Interruptions
- Interruption < 2 weeks: Continue treatment at the point stopped
- Interruption ≥ 2 weeks: May need to restart or perform drug susceptibility testing
- Source: https://medicalguidelines.msf.org/en/node/1671

The concern is about **complete missed doses** (hours → days), not same-day delays of <2 hours.

## Critical vs Non-Critical Timing

| Scenario | Clinical Significance |
|----------|---------------------|
| Single dose, 30-60 min late same day | **Low.** Consistent with standard "take as soon as you remember" guidance |
| Repeated inconsistent timing over weeks | **Moderate.** Habit formation matters — same time daily is best practice for adherence |
| Complete missed dose (skip a day) | **High.** Missing doses increases risk of treatment failure and drug resistance |
| Multiple consecutive missed days | **Critical.** May require treatment restart or resistance testing |
| Treatment interruption > 14 days | **Critical.** Drug susceptibility testing may be needed per WHO/MSF guidelines |

## Study Evidence
- **PMC7175980** (Temporal Factors and Missed Doses): 95.9% of TB patients missed ≥1 dose; 71.4% of gaps were a single dose. The study found "suboptimal dosing implementation in the first month" was associated with higher discontinuation rates.
- **WHO TB Knowledge Sharing**: "Daily" is defined as "at least five times per week" for TB treatment — acknowledging that occasional missed days are a realistic operational target.

## Caveats
- This is general TB treatment guideline info, not specific to the user's prescribed regimen
- Always defer to the prescribing doctor's specific instructions
- These are general references, not individualized medical advice
- Half-lives can vary with liver function (rifampin/isoniazid are hepatically metabolized), drug interactions, and individual metabolism
