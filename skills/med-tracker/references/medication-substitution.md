# Medication Substitution Verification

## When This Applies

A prescribed drug runs out and the user asks to substitute with an OTC/alternative product while on a complex multi-drug regimen (TB treatment + steroids + anticonvulsants in this case). This protocol covers the full interaction check.

## Methodology

### Phase 1: Risk of Going Without

- Determine clinical risk of missing 1-2 doses of the original drug
- Source: MSD Manual, published guidelines, not conjecture
- Example: Pyridoxine 25-50mg daily for INH neuropathy — missing 1-2 days is **low risk** (neuropathy is cumulative over weeks/months)

### Phase 2: Verify the Substitute

- Confirm the substitute's **active ingredient** and **dosage per unit**
- Compare against the **clinical recommendation** from trusted sources (MSD Manual, guidelines)
- Example: Swisse Ultiboost B-Complex = 41.1mg Pyridoxine/tablet. MSD Manual says 25-50mg daily for INH prophylaxis. ✓

### Phase 3: Whole-Regimen Interaction Check (Critical)

**Do NOT just check the substitute vs the drug it's replacing.** Trace through EVERY drug the user takes across their entire regimen. The user will push back ("CONFIRM EH?") if they sense you're only looking at one angle.

For each component of the substitute, check against EVERY concurrent drug:

| Substitue component | Drug | Check |
|---|---|---|
| B6 (Pyridoxine) 41.1mg | INH (Akurit-4) | Standard co-administration — INH causes B6 deficiency, B6 prevents it ✅ |
| B6 (Pyridoxine) 41.1mg | Rifampicin (Akurit-4) | No CYP interaction — B6 metabolised via non-CYP pathways ✅ |
| B6 (Pyridoxine) 41.1mg | Levetiracetam | No known interaction at 41mg. Some evidence B6 reduces levetiracetam side effects ✅ |
| B6 (Pyridoxine) 41.1mg | Dexamethasone | Steroids increase B6 excretion — supplementation is **beneficial** ✅ |
| B6 (Pyridoxine) 41.1mg | Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol | No interaction ✅ |
| B3 (Nicotinamide) 100mg | INH (Akurit-4) | **Protective** — INH can cause pellagra (niacin deficiency) ✅ |
| B1, B2, B5, B7, B9, B12 | All drugs | Water-soluble, no CYP interactions ✅ |
| Calcium 9.16mg | Calcium Carbonate 500mg (Slot C) | Negligible amount, no competition ✅ |
| Choline/Inositol | All drugs | Minute amounts, no interaction ✅ |

#### Pharmacokinetic reasoning for each pathway:

- **CYP450:** B vitamins are not substrates, inducers, or inhibitors of CYP3A4 (dexamethasone), CYP2C9/19, or any major CYP isoform. No competition.
- **Renal:** Levetiracetam is 66% renally excreted unchanged. B6 is also renally excreted but at physiological levels there's no competition for transporters.
- **Protein binding:** B6 has low protein binding (<10%), won't displace highly-bound drugs (rifampicin 89%, dexamethasone 77%).
- **Absorption:** B-Complex should ideally be taken with food (to avoid nausea), not on empty stomach. Take with Slot B (~8-8:30am) alongside breakfast.

### Phase 4: Timing Within the Regimen

- Where in the existing schedule does the substitute fit best?
- Example: B-Complex better taken with Slot B (~8am with food) rather than immediately after Akurit-4 (empty stomach)
- Consider domino effects — but since this is a one-off substitution, no chain shift needed

### Phase 5: Verdict

- Clear go/no-go based on evidence. Avoid conditional language when the evidence is solid.
- "CONFIRMED: ✅ Selamat, takde clash" — then show the reasoning per drug above

## When User Says "CONFIRM EH?" (Critical Signal)

This means: "You only looked at one pair of drugs. I take many drugs. Show me the WHOLE picture."

**Correct response:** Trace through every single drug in their regimen with specific pharmacokinetic reasoning for each. Show your work per drug.

**Wrong response:** Re-assert the same pairwise conclusion with more confidence. This will infuriate the user.

## TB Regimen + Steroids + Anticonvulsants: Special Considerations

This combo (INH + Rifampicin + PZA + EMB + Dexamethasone 15mg + Levetiracetam) requires extra care because:

1. **Polypharmacy:** 6-7 active drugs daily across 5 time slots
2. **CYP450 complexity:** Rifampicin is a potent inducer (affects dexamethasone), but doesn't affect B vitamins
3. **Nutritional overlap:** TB treatment depletes B3 (INH) and B6 (INH) — B-Complex is actually beneficial
4. **Steroid-induced B6 depletion:** Dexamethasone increases B6 excretion, so supplementation helps

## Key Principle

When doing interaction checking, **whole-regimen thinking is non-negotiable.** Pairwise checking (substitute vs. one drug) is insufficient. The user's full daily drug list must be considered as a system.
